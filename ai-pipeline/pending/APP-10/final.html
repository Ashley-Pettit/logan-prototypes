    How it works. The page scores each part of every vehicle and machine from hours or kilometres since its last service, telematics fault codes, oil sample results, prestart defect reports and breakdowns in the last year, and predicts roughly when it will need work. It then fills the workshop's next four weeks so tag-outs and the riskiest work happen first. When a fault comes in, AI ranks the likely causes from the fault codes, symptoms and the asset's repair history, lists the checks to do first, drafts a work order and books the repair into the plan. A fleet officer checks everything before it goes to the workshop.
    Prototype handover notes
    • In the LCC stack for production: HTML, CSS and JavaScript front end (move to Vue and Bootstrap) on Azure App Service, an Azure Function App for scoring and model calls, SQL for service history and plans, Data Factory or Logic Apps to bring in telematics and oil sample results, Entra ID sign-in.

    • Privacy: operator names in defect reports are swapped for placeholders before anything goes to AI. Only asset type, readings, codes and symptoms are sent.
    • The risk score, service intervals, fault code library, workshop capacity and costs are samples. They must be replaced with the manufacturers' schedules and City Fleet's own history and rates, then tested against past breakdowns.
    • Still needed for production: links to the council's fleet management system and the telematics platform, a test plan with an AI evaluation set of past faults, Dev, Test and Prod environments, and ICT-DA, CAB and business owner approvals.
